How can the non-stick performance of a coating be measured?

Part II · Experimental evaluation of non-stick performance and durability

Guillermo Guerrero Vacas · Department of Mechanical Engineering · University of Córdoba

18 November 2026 · Part II of II · Technical and scientific outreach · Leer en español

The real test of a non-stick coating is how easily the contacting material can be removed and whether this performance is retained throughout use.

← Read Part I: fundamentals and current technology

1. From perception to measurement

When food lifts easily from a frying pan or a part leaves a mould with little effort, we are observing non-stick performance. This qualitative observation is not sufficient to compare coatings or select an industrial solution.

As discussed in Part I, wettability provides information about liquid interactions, but cannot alone predict the release of a cooked, cured or solidified material. The contact conditions must be reproduced and separation measured.

2. The apparent contradiction of non-stick coatings

A non-stick coating must satisfy two requirements: a strong bond to its substrate and low resistance to separation from the material being released.

ISO 2409 specifies a cross-cut test for assessing resistance to detachment of a coating; ISO 4624 measures the tensile resistance of the coating system. Neither directly quantifies release performance against food, resins or foams. [1] [2]

4. What should be measured?

An instrumented separation test records the force–displacement curve, revealing both the maximum force and the mechanical work involved in separation.

σsep = Fmax / A0   ·   Wsep = ∫ F(δ) dδ

The first expression gives a nominal stress based on the initial contact area. The second is the area under the force curve over the separation interval. Dividing by the nominal area gives work per unit area; in peel tests, force per unit width is also commonly used.

The measured work may include deformation, friction and dissipation. It is not necessarily the thermodynamic work of adhesion. [8] The failure location should also be recorded: at the interface, within the released material or within the coating itself.

5. Durability: how long is non-stick performance retained?

Excellent initial release performance does not guarantee that it will be retained over repeated use. Abrasion, service temperatures, cleaning and chemical exposure can change the surface.

Durability can be assessed by measuring release at defined stages of a representative use programme. An application-specific acceptance threshold makes it possible to estimate functional service life: the number of cycles during which the system meets process requirements. If the threshold is not reached, the test only establishes a demonstrated minimum life.

6. Our research experience at the University of Córdoba

Non-stick performance in food processing

A 2024 study developed a procedure using a pancake-like model food cooked on coated aluminium substrates. After 90 cooking cycles, the fluoropolymer separation stresses were around 3 kPa, compared with approximately 15–34 kPa for the ceramic systems studied. [4]

Sol-gel ceramics versus PTFE

In 2025, we compared five commercial ceramic formulations against PTFE, assessing model-food separation, water droplet mobility and wear resistance. After 90 cooking cycles, PTFE retained lower and more stable release resistance; some ceramic coatings showed useful behaviour against particular wear mechanisms. This is not a universal ranking of technologies. [5]

Polyurethane foam mould release

Polyurethane foam manufacturing provides an example of industrial transfer. In 2018, different coatings were tested on aluminium moulds and selected solutions were transferred to industrial conditions. One PFA coating achieved around 1,500 production cycles without significant incidents under the conditions studied. [6] Later work examined interactions between polymeric mould materials and release agents. [7]

7. A methodology for evaluating coatings

Evaluation should start with real application needs and end with a functional acceptance criterion. Alongside temperature, pressure, speed and contact time, specimen geometry, preparation and cleaning conditions must be controlled.

Independent specimens, a reference surface and reporting of measurement variability are recommended. Repeated cycles on one specimen describe its evolution but are not independent replicates.

8. Final remarks

The transition to new surfaces calls for objective, representative testing. The best solution is not necessarily the one offering the easiest first release, but the one maintaining adequate performance over the service life required by the process.
