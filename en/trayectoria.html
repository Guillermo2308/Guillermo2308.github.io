Guillermo Guerrero Vacas is an Associate Professor (Profesor Titular de Universidad) in Manufacturing Engineering at the Department of Mechanics, Polytechnic School of Córdoba, University of Córdoba. He holds a PhD from the University of Málaga, a degree in Mechanical Engineering and a Master's degree in Manufacturing Engineering. He has been involved in university teaching since 1992 and has held his current tenured faculty position since 2017. His academic responsibilities have included heading the Department of Mechanics.

His research focuses on manufacturing processes and material behaviour, particularly surface engineering and functional coatings: non-stick performance, adhesion and mold release, wettability, surface characterization, and the durability of materials under service conditions. These investigations address functional surfaces in molds, tools and manufactured components.

He also studies advanced manufacturing, including machining and process optimization, metal and polymer additive manufacturing, hybrid processes, and single-point incremental forming (SPIF). His work combines experimental design, metrology, mechanical and surface testing, and quantitative analysis to understand product performance.

More recently, his research has expanded into sustainable materials, especially the technological use of sheep wool in composites and potential thermal-insulation applications. This interdisciplinary area connects materials engineering with circular-economy challenges and collaboration in animal production and food technology.

His scientific activity includes 46 articles in JCR-indexed journals, according to the academic records updated in November 2026. He has received two recognized six-year research assessment periods and has contributed to funded research projects, national and international scientific meetings, four patents and knowledge-transfer activities.

His teaching spans manufacturing technology, metrology, quality control, and industrial maintenance at bachelor's and master's levels, with an emphasis on practical engineering, industrial examples and digital learning tools.

Across these activities, the unifying goal is to develop measurable and reproducible technological solutions through experimental science and collaboration between academia and industry.
